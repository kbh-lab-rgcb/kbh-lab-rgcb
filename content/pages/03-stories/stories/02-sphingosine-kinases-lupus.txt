title: Two kinases, two outcomes — pinpointing SphK1 in lupus
tag: Autoimmunity · Lipid signalling
caption: SphK2 deficiency enhances pristane-induced apoptosis. (A) Peritoneal-cell apoptosis by flow cytometry. (B) Renal IgG deposition. (C) Serum IgG autoantibodies. p < 0.05, p < 0.005, P < 0.001.

lead: A paired investigation of SphK2 and SphK1 showed that closely related
  enzymes can play very different roles in interferon-driven autoimmunity, an
  important distinction when choosing a therapeutic target.
why: Comparing related enzymes prevented a misleading class-wide conclusion.
  SphK2 influenced cell development without changing disease, whereas SphK1
  controlled the pathogenic activation programme that drives interferon-linked
  autoimmunity.
excerpt: Side-by-side studies separated dendritic-cell development from
  disease-driving activation in lupus, identifying SphK1 — not SphK2 — as the
  enzyme linked to interferon production and kidney inflammation.
papers: 10.1096/fj.201802535R, 10.1096/fj.201902847R

Systemic lupus erythematosus is marked by an overactive type I interferon
response that can damage multiple organs. Plasmacytoid dendritic cells are
central to this process, because they produce large amounts of interferon after
endosomal Toll-like receptors 7 and 9 detect nucleic acids. Our studies asked
whether the two sphingosine kinases that generate the signalling lipid
sphingosine 1-phosphate — SphK1 and SphK2 — influence this pathway in the same
way.

The SphK2 study produced a useful negative result. Loss of SphK2 increased
markers of plasmacytoid dendritic cells, enhanced their differentiation and
raised expression of TLR7 and TLR9. Yet SphK2 deficiency did not alter the
initiation or progression of pristane-induced lupus in mice. Although the
frequency of these cells increased, their activation state changed little, and
SphK2 expression was not altered in patients with lupus. The findings suggested
a role in dendritic-cell development but argued against SphK2 inhibition as a
likely lupus therapy.

SphK1 told a different story. Its loss did not prevent plasmacytoid
dendritic-cell development, but it sharply impaired cell activation and the
production of type I interferon and pro-inflammatory cytokines after TLR7/9
stimulation. Genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition reduced uptake of
TLR9 ligands and, in the pristane model, lowered the interferon signature,
plasmacytoid dendritic-cell activation and glomerulonephritis. SphK1 expression
and sphingosine 1-phosphate levels were also elevated in patients with lupus.
Read together, the studies narrow the therapeutic hypothesis from a broad enzyme
family to a specific SphK1-dependent checkpoint, while remaining preclinical.
