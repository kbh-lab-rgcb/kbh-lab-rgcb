title: Turning immune-cold colorectal tumours hot by targeting acid ceramidase
tag: Cancer metabolism · Immunotherapy
caption: Overview of the research
lead: Blocking acid ceramidase reshaped tumour-cell metabolism, triggered
  immunogenic cell death and strengthened anti-PD-1 responses in preclinical
  colorectal cancer models.
why: The work connects sphingolipid metabolism to immune escape and offers a
  two-part strategy: stress the tumour cell from within, while making the
  surrounding microenvironment more responsive to immunotherapy.
excerpt: Targeting acid ceramidase exposed a metabolic weakness in colorectal
  cancer and made tumours more visible to the immune system. Combined with
  anti-PD-1 therapy, ASAH1 inhibition increased interferon signalling and
  cytotoxic T-cell infiltration.
papers: 10.1016/j.jare.2023.12.013

Checkpoint immunotherapy depends on an active antitumour immune response, yet
many colorectal tumours remain immunologically cold: they contain too few
effective cytotoxic T cells and are surrounded by signals that suppress
immunity. We investigated whether sphingolipid metabolism helps maintain this
resistant state, focusing on acid ceramidase (ASAH1), the enzyme that converts
ceramide into sphingosine.

The study found that ASAH1 was overexpressed in human colorectal cancer samples
and in mouse tumours. Genetic silencing or pharmacological inhibition with
LCL-521 pushed cancer cells towards immunogenic cell death — a form of death
that alerts the immune system rather than remaining invisible to it. ASAH1
blockade also disrupted the glutathione antioxidant system, increased reactive
oxygen species and produced mitochondrial stress, linking a metabolic
vulnerability inside tumour cells to the signals that recruit and activate
immunity.

In an immune-competent colorectal cancer model, LCL-521 reduced tumour growth as
a single agent and enhanced the effect of anti-PD-1 treatment. The response
included type I and type II interferon signalling, activation of M1-like
macrophages and T cells, greater infiltration of cytotoxic CD8-positive T cells,
and suppression of myeloid-derived suppressor cells and regulatory T cells. In
effect, ASAH1 inhibition helped convert a cold tumour microenvironment into a
more immune-reactive one. These preclinical findings establish acid ceramidase
as a druggable metabolic target and support further development of ASAH1
inhibitors in combination with checkpoint therapy.
