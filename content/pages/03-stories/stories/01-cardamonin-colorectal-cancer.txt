title: From a spice-derived molecule to a colorectal cancer programme
tag: Natural products · Colorectal cancer
lead: Across two preclinical studies we traced how cardamonin can interrupt both
  tumour formation and inflammation-driven colorectal cancer, in part by
  reshaping microRNA networks.
why: The work shows how a natural-product observation can become a mechanistic
  programme: first demonstrating antitumour activity, then testing whether the
  same molecule can interrupt the chronic inflammation that helps drive
  colorectal cancer.
excerpt: Two linked studies reveal how cardamonin, a spice-derived chalcone,
  suppresses tumour-promoting and inflammatory pathways in preclinical
  colorectal cancer models.
papers: 10.1038/s41598-017-14253-8, 10.3390/biom11050661

Colorectal cancer can emerge through more than one route. Some tumours develop
as epithelial cells gradually acquire malignant features; others arise in the
setting of long-standing intestinal inflammation. Our cardamonin work connected
these routes through a single question: could a naturally occurring chalcone
from the ginger family influence the molecular programmes that allow colorectal
tumours to form and progress?

In the first study we tested cardamonin in an azoxymethane-induced mouse model
of colorectal cancer. Treatment began either when carcinogen exposure started or
after early lesions were expected to have formed. In both settings cardamonin
reduced tumour burden and was associated with lower cell proliferation, reduced
β-catenin and NF-κB signalling, and no apparent toxicity at the doses tested.
Experiments in human colorectal cancer cell lines extended the finding:
cardamonin slowed growth, promoted cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis, and altered
cellular redox balance. Global profiling also showed coordinated changes in
microRNAs, suggesting a mechanism broader than a single signalling target.

The follow-up study moved into the inflammation–cancer continuum. Cardamonin
dampened inflammatory responses in activated macrophages, reducing nitric oxide,
iNOS, TNF-α, IL-6 and NF-κB activity. It protected mice in a model of
experimental colitis and reduced tumour formation in a model of
colitis-associated colorectal cancer. Once again, microRNA analysis linked the
response to networks that regulate inflammation and tumorigenesis. Together the
studies define a research arc from chemoprevention to inflammation-associated
disease, and position cardamonin as a preclinical lead for deeper mechanistic,
pharmacological and safety evaluation — not yet as a clinical treatment or a
dietary recommendation.
